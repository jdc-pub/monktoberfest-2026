== 2021: GPT-2 / GPT-3

[source,julia,role=tiny]

function GPT3(prompt::String; filename, kwargs...)
    response = let
      choice = first( <1>
        openai.Completion.create(; kwargs..., n=1)["choices"])
      response = choice["text"] |> string
    end

    open(filename, "w") do io
        write(io,
            """
            This file was autogenerated with GPT-3! All content <2>
            is checked for obscenities, and no prompts should
            produce any inappropriate content. Still, if you
            find ANY offensive content in this file, please
            contact the project's maintainer (@cadojo on GitHub).

            This file has the following format: <prompt>... <response>

            ---

            $prompt... $response <3>

            """
        )
    end

    return response
end
----

== Plagiarism via ChatGPT

* *Fall 2022 Discussion Post*
** _I agree with Mark's comment about the text lacking pathos._

[.fragment]
* *Spring 2023 Discussion Post*
** _I see what you mean, but I'd push back a little on the idea that the text lacks pathos — I think the emotional appeal is there, just operating more quietly than we might expect. Pathos doesn't have to mean vivid sob stories or dramatic language; sometimes an author builds emotion through understatement, word choice, or even a deliberately cold, clinical tone that makes the reader feel *more* unsettled, not less. When you say the piece feels emotionally flat, I'd encourage you to ask whether that flatness is an absence of pathos or a strategic choice aimed at a specific audience—maybe readers who'd distrust anything that feels too manipulative. For me, the most affecting moment was actually the most restrained one, which suggests the author trusts the audience to supply the feeling themselves._

[.notes]
--
- I saw the sentiments in creatives change after ChatGPT was released, in November 2022.
- My wife was in an MFA program at that time, and so I was learning a lot about how writing programs work.
- Specifically, TAs in STEM were kind of like professor assistants — but that is not how it works in the writing world.
- When you're a TA, you're a full professor: you're making the lesson plans, leading the lectures, creating the assignments, and grading them.
- I remember in that first fall semester, before ChatGPT was released, how hard it is sometimes to get undergraduates to write substantive discussion posts.
- I remember my own wimpy discussion posts when I was an undergrad.
- Before ChatGPT was released, there was a percentage of comments that would be like this: "I agree with Mark."
- Okay, can we get a little bit more? It's kind of like pulling teeth.
- In the spring, which is the first semester that started with ChatGPT, you would see a lot fewer of those comments.
- Instead, you would see this.
- You can't prove it, but it's clear that some number of students were not writing in their how-to-write class.
- And I heard the graduate students in this program talking about this — talking about writing centers, and grading, and helping undergraduates write.
- This was insulting.
- Many universities encouraged the use of these tools — ChatGPT in particular — and did not provide guidance or tools to professors for policing plagiarism or enforcing their plagiarism policies.
- I don't think the sentiment that many creatives had towards AI recovered from this point.
- This ended up being more than an annoyance: the animators and writers who I know started to have a very negative view of artificial intelligence after it came out, after the public got a better understanding of how these products were built.

- And I then saw a lot of comments on that backlash from folks within the tech industry that I would say were unhelpful.

== Version Control

_Casey's work._

[.notes]
--
- Because so many of the versioning systems are tied in with platforms in order to recover unsaved documents.
- In Microsoft Word, you have to go through OneDrive, and now your file doesn't exist in your normal Documents folder — it exists in OneDrive.
- Now, Word does have autosave, but it takes over a minute to save a single document, and it forces you to save things in OneDrive.
- When Microsoft Word crashes — I haven't had an editor crash that's caused me to lose work in I can't even remember how long — this still happens for creatives.
- And they have to go to a Program Files folder and find a cryptically named file, open it not in the default application but in a text editor, so that they can recover the lost work.
- That's an abomination.
- And I think we can do a lot of good bringing version control to creatives.

== Ask Creatives!

- Talk to creatives about how they work.
- No chat bots in the application!
- Avoid leaking details behind your abstractions.

[.notes]
--
- But of course, we have to ask creatives — talk to creatives about how they work.
- Ask them what they're missing.
- Ask them what's annoying about their workflows, propose things, and see what they think.
- I will go out on a limb and say: please don't add a chatbot.
- I would really be surprised if you find a substantial user base interested in that.
- And avoid leaking the details behind your abstractions.
- I learned about tree-sitter this last year, and I'm not proud of this.

- You can see here in the Zed editor that the concrete syntax tree is viewable, it updates in real time, and it's really efficient on every document.
- And we could have that — we could even have color coding for the different clauses, and versioning for the different clauses.
- And she was like, "What the fuck are you talking about?"

== Case: Interstellar

[.notes]
--
- One of my favorite movies is Interstellar, and it's a perfect example of this.
- Creatives asked technologists — in this case, scientists — for the best possible simulations of a massive black hole.
- And in the process of doing that, creatives got this great simulation — a big box office hit.
- It's a great movie.
- And scientists, when working on this problem that they wouldn't have worked on otherwise, found new science.
- This is a new problem domain that we could enter, and I think we would find a lot of benefits there.
- For writing in particular, we've got a lot of writing workflows today.
- And so we could use some better prose-writing tools.
